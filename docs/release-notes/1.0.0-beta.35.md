**Four places where AI17Z's own records stopped matching what it had done.** All four were found by running the live installations rather than by reading the code, and three of them had already cost something.

## It had stopped posting, and the reason was a jam rather than judgement

A post's identity is anchored to the idea behind it and never expires, so once a job exists for an idea that is permanent. When an owner rejected a draft, the idea went back into the backlog unchanged, won the next selection because it still scored highest, found its job again, and stopped. Every six hours, for twelve days, while a newer idea sat behind it untouched.

Releasing it charged nothing either, so the guard against an unpublishable idea blocking the queue could never fire: it counts attempts, and attempts stayed at zero.

The idea now names the job that took it, which takes it out of the running immediately and hands it to the reconciler to settle by asking that job how it went. Published means used. Rejected charges an attempt and records why. Enough of those set it aside with the reason kept. An owner's rejection stays a rejection, and nothing is deleted or quietly retried.

Silence from an agent is supposed to mean it had nothing worth saying. It can mean that again.

## Twelve likes it recorded and never sent

The worse one. The part of AI17Z that performs a remote action reported two completely different endings the same way: a rehearsal that verified its target and stopped, and a claim that another worker was already holding. The only thing separating them was a sentence written for a model to read.

Engagement read the second as success. On a live account: twelve rows recorded as done, twelve jobs marked executed, and nothing sent to X. The daily ceiling counts what was done, so those twelve filled a ceiling of twelve, and the next four candidates it genuinely wanted to act on were declined for being over the limit. The account stopped engaging for a day while its own history said it was working.

The ending is a value now rather than a sentence. A claim it could not get is waiting, not done, and the attempt is given back, because nothing was asked of X.

Underneath it was the reason those claims were stuck in the first place: verifying a target can fail, and only the sending was protected, so a browser problem left the action marked in flight for ever. Sixteen of them had built up. That is closed, and the two endings that had never cleared their own in-flight rows now do what the other two always did.

## An installation whose X session had expired still said it was fine

The reading layer had its half right. When X asks for a sign-in it recognises that, stops rather than loading the same page again behind a session that is not coming back, and says so to whoever asked.

It told nothing else. The account went on reporting connected, the health row went on saying X is read through the signed-in browser, and the only thing that could correct either was an owner pressing a health check by hand, because nothing schedules one. An installation in that state looks healthy while everything it watches quietly returns nothing.

There was already a state for this, and the owner notification already reads it. It just had a single writer that nothing ever ran. Every X read records it now. Only from connected, because an account that never had a session is a different thing, and a rate limit is left alone rather than sending somebody to re-authenticate an account that works.

## And nobody was told when the model stopped answering

The notification for a provider refusing calls was complete: a severity chosen deliberately, a body naming the provider and what it said, a link to the right screen, and protection against repeating itself. Nothing called it. A provider failing reached an owner only if they happened to be looking at the Health screen.

AI17Z runs on somebody's own machine, which is exactly why it has notifications. It is raised from the same place the Health screen reads, so the two cannot disagree. An untested provider is not a failing one, and a credential the owner switched off clears rather than nags.

## Smaller things

**The Response Lab counts repeated openings the way the product compares them**, instead of on raw text, where a straight apostrophe and a curly one made seven identical openings look like four and three. It also says out loud that a rehearsal publishes nothing, so the guard it appears to be reporting on had no history to work from and the number is the model's raw tendency rather than what would be sent.
