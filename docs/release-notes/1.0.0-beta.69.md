`v1.0.0-beta.69` gives an agent, and anybody who calls the shared runtime, three
ways to ask before acting: what an EVM call would do, what a Solana transaction
would do, and whether a trade would pass the risk gate. None of them signs,
sends or journals anything.

## What an EVM call would do

`transaction.inspect` decodes the calls that move a signer's tokens or let
somebody else move them, exactly and with integer arithmetic: `approve`,
`increaseAllowance`, `transfer`, `transferFrom`, `setApprovalForAll` and
Permit2's `approve`, and any native value. An allowance at or above half the
largest integer is unlimited and needs a person. Approve-all needs a person.
Give the spenders, recipients, tokens and value you expect, and anything
outside them is refused with the reason.

**Nothing is signed blind.** A function it cannot read, or a known selector
with the wrong number of arguments, needs a person. Call data that is not a
selector followed by whole 32-byte words is refused.

## What a Solana transaction would do

`transaction.inspect_solana` parses the serialized transaction itself, legacy
or version 0, and reads the System transfer and the SPL Token transfer,
transfer-checked, approve and approve-checked. It names the fee payer and the
signatures required, says an approval of the largest amount is unlimited, and
refuses a fee payer, recipient, delegate, program or lamport total outside
what you expected.

Anything it cannot read needs a person: a program not on its short list, an
instruction it does not know, or an account from an address lookup table,
which cannot be resolved without asking the chain. So a program it mistakes
can only make the answer more cautious.

## Would this trade pass

`trading.preflight` takes a trade and the mandate it would act under, reads
the market for the exact assets, works out the floor the way a paper trade
does, and asks `judgeTrade`, the gate a real trade passes: ALLOW,
APPROVAL_REQUIRED or DENY with every reason and the quote it was judged
against. It says what it assumed: a real trade must also pass a simulation,
which a preflight does not run, and exposure you did not give is counted as
nothing.

## A backtest says whether a failed read is worth retrying

The first live backtest after a paper trade found the market source's budget
already spent and answered a bare failure. The read's own outcome now travels
with `retryable`, so a source that is briefly refusing is waited on and a
request that was wrong is not retried.

## Upgrading

Nothing to do. No migration, no setting.
