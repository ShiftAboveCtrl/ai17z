**Your agent had answered eighty-five people and the screen said it had answered nobody.** That is fixed, along with the four other faults found underneath it: mentions dated wrong by up to forty-four hours, a discovery monitor that reported a broken page as a quiet one, a replies monitor that had read nothing for eight days while showing a green light, and two thousand posts a day being put through the full reply pipeline in order to decide not to reply to them.

## Replied was empty, and it should not have been

Activity → Replied showed nothing. The agent had in fact replied eighty-five times.

The list was fetching the most recent rows and *then* filtering them by state. That is harmless while recent rows are a mixture, and they were not: with the radar watching three keywords, the most recent two hundred events were every single one of them a keyword match. You had to go back hundreds of events to reach the most recent reply. Four hundred and forty-nine when this was found, five hundred and eighty-four a few hours later. So asking for "Replied" looked at forty rows that could not contain one and answered honestly that there were none. The counts above the list were computed the same way, so they agreed with it and were wrong in the same direction.

Both now ask the database the actual question. Filtering happens before the limit, and the counts are over everything rather than over a window.

## A new filter: who actually wrote to you

Activity now opens on **Sent to this agent**, meaning mentions, replies and direct messages only, with a press to include everything the radar found.

These are not comparable quantities. Over one seventy-two hour period this installation recorded 2,058 posts found by watching keywords and fifteen messages from actual people. A list holding both is a list of the first.

## Mentions were dated wrong, by up to forty-four hours

X does not always render a timestamp on a notification. When it did not, AI17Z recorded the moment it happened to be looking as the time the post was written.

That field is what decides whether something is too old to answer, so the consequences ran both ways within the same minute. Coming back from being switched off for two days, this installation answered a mention that was twenty-three hours old as though it had just arrived, and in the same poll refused a three-hour-old one as stale, because that one's timestamp happened to be readable.

A post's identifier contains the time it was written. AI17Z now reads it from there, which is exact rather than approximate: checked against three posts where X did render a timestamp, the rendered time and the calculated one agreed to the minute on all three.

## A broken page is no longer reported as a quiet one

When X cannot render a page it says "Something went wrong. Try reloading." Every reader in AI17Z checks for that and says so. Every reader except the radar's own monitors, which are the ones that run unattended.

So a monitor could load a broken page, find no posts, and be recorded as a healthy poll that found nothing. That is why one screen could show an error while another showed a healthy green source: the one showing the error was telling the truth.

Monitors now refuse an empty page that X has admitted it could not draw. The source goes amber, backs off, keeps its place, and shows what X said. A surface that genuinely has nothing on it is still reported as quiet.

## The replies monitor had read nothing for eight days

The monitor that reads replies under your own posts occasionally spends a cycle reading your account's follower count instead. It decided whether that was due by asking when the last reading was *stored*, and a reading that fails stores nothing.

So the first failure made it permanently due. For eight days it took that branch every three minutes, failed, recorded a healthy zero, and returned without looking at a single thread. No error, no warning, a green light throughout.

It now counts the attempt rather than the result, so a failure costs one cycle exactly like a success does.

## Sources say why they have nothing to show

A source with no error and no results was ambiguous, and that ambiguity is what hid the fault above. "I checked and found nothing" and "there was nothing for me to check" are different facts about your account, and the Radar panel now says which.

## Who wrote to you is polled before what you are watching

A poll costs a page load on one signed-in browser, so the monitors compete for something real and scarce. Ordered only by which came due first, a keyword search could be polled ahead of a mention, and on this installation the direct monitors were running four to six minutes late while the keyword monitors were not yet due at all.

Mentions, replies and your own threads are now taken first. Watching a keyword is the agent browsing; somebody who wrote to you is waiting.

## Watched keywords no longer cost a full reply for every post

Over seventy-two hours, 2,058 keyword matches produced 1,946 complete pipeline runs. 1,855 of them were cancelled and **none of them published anything at all**. Each one loaded the post's page, sent every picture on it to a vision model, and assembled context, all to reach a conclusion the deterministic rules at the end could have reached from the text. The vision model was called 566 times in that window.

The same judgement now runs first, on what is already known. Critically it is *the same function*, asked with every unknown set to whatever would most favour replying, so it can only decline things the full run would also have declined. Your daily approach limit and your per-person cooldown are checked there too, because those are the last things worth a pipeline run to discover.

Replayed against that seventy-two hours, 1,655 of the 1,857 cancelled runs, 89 per cent, would never have been created. Everything seen is still recorded, still in Activity, and still yours to act on.

Nothing that anybody sends your agent goes through this. A mention or a reply gets the full pipeline whatever it scores, because somebody asked.

## Waiting for you is now at most fifteen things

This installation had seventy-one decisions waiting. Every one was a suggestion to approach a stranger found by a keyword search, and not one was a message from a person. If somebody had written in and their reply had needed your judgement, it would have been item seventy-two.

There is now a **Waiting for you** view holding at most fifteen, in the order the decisions deserve: a security check first, then somebody who wrote to you, then anything that cannot be taken back, then the agent's own decisions, then suggestions. No single kind may take more than six places, so a flood of one thing cannot bury everything else.

Nothing is deleted. What does not fit is counted and described beneath the list, and comes forward as you answer what is shown. Several suggestions about approaching the same person are folded into one, because that is one decision asked repeatedly, and your per-person cooldown would refuse the second approach anyway. Requests whose post has been deleted, or that have been overtaken, stop taking a place. They are described as expired or superseded, which is what they are, rather than as failed.

## Waiting for you stops filling up

An agent set to show unprompted approaches before sending them publishes none of them, and the daily approach limit only ever counted what was published. So the limit read zero for ever, and every candidate that cleared the bar added another proposal to the pile. That is how one hundred and five decisions came to be waiting for an agent permitted five approaches a day.

The same allowance now applies to what is outstanding. Once there are as many unanswered approach proposals as the agent may make in a day, it stops writing more and says so. Answering any of them makes room immediately.

This never applies to somebody who wrote to you. A mention or a reply waiting for your judgement is not an approach, and nothing here holds one back.

## Late messages are answered rather than dropped, and not all at once

A mention or reply may now be up to a day old and still be answered; a post the radar merely came across is still held to two hours. Sixteen real messages had been recorded and never considered, every one of them because AI17Z was slow to find them. Being late to answer somebody is the agent's fault, not theirs. Of those sixteen, eight are within the new window; the other eight are more than a day old and stay recorded and visible rather than answered.

Answering a day-late message is right. Answering twelve of them within six minutes of each other is a machine working through a backlog, and the thirty-second spacing between actions is no protection against that. So catch-up is spaced out: anything found well after it was written waits behind whatever is already queued, and each one re-checks the conversation, the person and the freshness when its turn comes. Anything that has just arrived still goes first, which is almost every message.
