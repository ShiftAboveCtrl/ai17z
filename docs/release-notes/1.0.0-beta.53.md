**No agent should spend its day in a cooldown, an agent that goes looking for people now finds people, and every agent now learns from what happens to what it publishes.** A platform-wide pace for asking X replaces the fast defaults every account was created with, one page X failed to draw no longer cools a whole account down, an agent that follows somebody spends most of its growth sessions on the people who talk to that account, and each agent tests what it learns before it keeps it.

## One pace for asking X, for every account

Every X account used to be created checking its notifications every 30 seconds, searching its mentions every 60 and its replies every 90. Measured on a live installation, the account on those defaults hit X's "something went wrong" on both searches about every two hours and spent a day in and out of cooldown, while an account checking every 5 and 10 minutes read nearly twice as much in total and was never refused once. Search is what X guards.

- **A default and a floor for each kind of page.** Notifications every 90 seconds (never faster than 60), mentions search every 5 minutes, replies search every 10, your agent's own threads and watched accounts every 5 (never faster than 3), watched keywords every 30 minutes (never faster than 15).
- **Your settings still count, in one direction.** You can make any source slower. A setting faster than the floor runs at the floor, and the Radar panel shows the pace each source really runs at and says when yours was raised.
- **Existing accounts move to the new pace on their own.** Nothing to change.

## One page X could not draw is not a cooldown

X's own "something went wrong" page counted as pressure on the whole account. An account recovering from a cooldown re-entered one on the next such page, and with recovery lasting up to two hours and a page like that about every two hours, it never got out: its strikes climbed from 5 to 9 in a day.

- **One such page now slows the source that saw it**, through that source's own backoff. Three or more together are still treated as X pushing back, and an explicit "slow down" from X still cools the account down at once.
- **Strikes are forgotten after six quiet hours**, so one bad evening no longer decides how long an account rests for days afterwards.

## Growth that talks to people

Topic searches on a crypto subject return mostly people selling coins, and a live agent spent its growth sessions correcting token pitches. An agent that follows somebody now looks where that person spends their own replies:

- **Replies nobody answered.** Your agent reads the replies under a recent post by an account you asked it to follow and answers the people that account never answered, briefly and warmly, in its own voice. It never answers somebody the account already answered, never replies as that account, and never answers the account itself this way. A reply like that stays answerable for twelve hours rather than the two a stranger gets, because the account it was written to usually posted hours earlier.
- **Their circle.** The people the followed account replies to again and again are learned from its own replies, and your agent reads their fresh posts and talks to them like somebody in that circle would.
- **Topics still count, a third of the time.** Two sessions in three are now about people, and one is a search of your agent's subjects.
- **Not annoying.** At most two people a session, one person once, nobody approached in the last week, never a second reply in the same thread, and never a pitch.

## Token pitches

- **A pitch is declined outright** when nobody asked, however much else a post scores. That now includes a call teased through a contract address, a price promised on a clock and a closing "want in?".
- **Under a milder promotional post, or when somebody tags your agent in their own**, it never endorses the token, repeats its ticker or contract, or talks about its price. If it points people at anything, it is AI17Z, in a few words.

## Discovery asks X only for what can still be answered

A search now asks X for posts from within the window your agent may answer, instead of filtering out old ones afterwards. Before, 8 to 10 of every 20 results were too old to use.

## Your agent learns from what happens, and checks what it learns

Every reply and post your agent published was already being revisited and measured, and nothing ever used the numbers. Now it learns from them, and does not trust what it learns until it has tested it.

- **Every reply and post is scored** about six hours after it goes out, from its views, likes, reposts, replies, quotes and bookmarks, against your agent's own recent results. A small account and a large one learn on the same scale.
- **The score is credited to the choices behind it:** how the post it answered was found (somebody in the replies of an account it follows, that account's circle, a topic search), how long it wrote, whether it asked something, and how large the author's audience was. Older results count for less, halving every two weeks.
- **A change is a test before it is a habit.** When one option clearly does better, your agent starts leaning into it while still running its old behaviour one time in five as a control. When the test has enough results it keeps the change if it did at least as well, and undoes it if it did worse.
- **It learns how far to trust itself.** Each choice it has been right about needs less evidence before the next change; each one it has been wrong about needs more. That is what makes the learning recursive rather than a counter.
- **It can only move what was already its choice.** Where it looks, whose posts it favours among ones that passed every rule, the length it aims for within your limit, whether it tends to ask. It can never change a permission, its identity rules, the rules about token pitches, anybody who asked to be left alone, your outreach limits or the quality checks.
- **Your own answers count.** When you turn down an approach to somebody, your agent does not queue another approach to them for a week. Your approvals and rejections were recorded before this and never read.
- **You can see all of it.** The Autonomy panel shows how each choice has been doing, what it is testing, what it kept or undid and why, and has a button to forget it all. Learning can be switched off per agent in Policies.

## Upgrade notes

One migration, adding where outcomes, evidence, tests and trust are kept. On its first wake each agent scores the last thirty days of what it published. Existing radar sources keep their settings; any faster than the new floor run at the floor, and the Radar panel says so.
