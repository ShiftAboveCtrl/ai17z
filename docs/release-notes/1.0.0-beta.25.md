**Four things an owner reported, all of them real, and the missing half of autonomy.** Each root cause below was measured on a live installation rather than guessed at.

---

## Chrome stops jumping in front of what you were doing

Every time AI17Z opened a tab, Chrome came forward and took the screen. Recycling made it worse, because recycling opens tabs on purpose.

The cause is one missing flag. Opening a tab goes out as a request Chrome interprets as "and bring this to the front". Measured against a minimised Chrome:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| before | window restored itself, **foreground stolen** from another application |
| after | window **stayed minimised**, foreground untouched |

The first attempt to measure this said "no change" both ways and was wrong: the window happened to be maximised on a second monitor, so Chrome had nothing to restore. Minimising it first is what reproduces what you actually see.

**Not fixed by minimising Chrome afterwards.** That steals the focus first and gives it back, which is worse than the problem.

Chrome still comes forward when it genuinely needs you: signing in, a security check, or when you ask for it.

## Replies finish their sentences

Replies were stopping around 170 characters, mid-thought:

> *"...a different risk class than"*
> *"...why no official X API key is"*

The limit was 280 the whole time. What was cutting them was a **second** ceiling inside the voice layer, worked out from how long the agent's own writing samples are. With no measured history yet, that comes from the persona's style examples, and this agent has thirty-two of them running from *"ha. okay that's fair"* upwards. Those show a **register**, not a length, and taking a maximum from them produced about 175.

Drafts of 196 to 230 characters were being published at 170 to 175.

Being longer than usual is now a reason to ask for a shorter draft, never a reason to cut a sentence in half. A truncated thought reads as broken; a long one only reads as long.

**And a sign-off the cutting was hiding.** With the chop gone, *"Hope that helps — let me know if you have any other questions!"* turned out to lose only its second half, leaving *"Hope that helps —"* dangling. Sign-offs now come off until none is left.

## Your agent can now like and repost things itself

It could already decide what to *say*. It had no way to decide a specific post was worth **acknowledging**. Every like went through the model happening to call for one mid-conversation. An agent that reads its timeline all day and cannot like anything is not participating, it is lurking.

**A like and a repost are different decisions.** A like says "worth reading" and costs the reader nothing. A repost spends the attention of everybody following your agent, on somebody else's words, with your agent's name on it, so it needs the subject **and** the substance, not merely a high total. Otherwise an account becomes a friend's amplifier rather than a reader.

**What it refuses is the point.** Engagement bait is turned down outright rather than scored low, because amplifying a post whose purpose is to be amplified is the definition of farming. So is its own post, one it already acted on, anybody you have blocked, anything over two days old, and anything on a subject it will not raise on its own. Quietly endorsing somebody else's post about an election is raising the subject with extra steps.

**Nothing acts without you until you say so.** Below **Act** it proposes and stops, and each suggestion shows the post, the reasons, and how sure it is. You approve or decline. Daily ceilings are deliberately low and lower still for reposts: an agent that likes forty things a day is farming, whatever its reasons said.

It runs through the same machinery a reply does: the same duplicate protection, the same check against X before retrying, the same audit trail.

## "4 messages are waiting for you to decide"

Beside an empty inbox, for over a week, with no way to dismiss it.

The count was never wrong. The **list** was incomplete. It showed conversations, meaning things people sent your agent, and all four waiting items were work the agent had started **itself**: an original post and three follow-ups. Counted by the badge, shown by nothing.

The list now includes work your agent is proposing, so what the badge points at is something you can actually reach and decide. Only the ones held for you: an agent posts on a schedule and most of those finish on their own.

## Controls that look like controls

The reach settings rendered as:

```
OnOff
allowedAsk
meOff
```

Three buttons with no space between them. They had no styling at all, and neither did any other choice control in AI17Z, because every one of them relied on a default that was never written. The accessibility was correct the whole time, which is exactly why nobody caught it: a screen reader heard a perfectly good set of options while a person saw three words stuck together.

## A place to try an agent before you trust it

A new **Lab** in Studio. Give it a message, or paste a link to a real post on X, and see what your agent would say to it. Nothing is published, and there is no setting that changes that.

The part worth opening it twice for is the second half. A draft on its own is something to like or dislike; a draft with the evidence beside it is something to correct. So it shows:

- **what it could see**: the message, who wrote it, the post above it, what it remembered, what it looked up, what it knows about that person. Anything absent is named rather than left blank.
- **how it got there**: every stage, and what each one decided. Including the decision not to answer, which is a decision here rather than a failure, and the one an owner least understands without the reasons beside it.

It runs the real pipeline rather than an approximation of it: the same context, memory, research, engagement heuristic, voice compiler and validator, stopping before the action. What you read is what actually happened, not an account of it.

A real post is read through the browser your account is already signed in to, with the post above it, so a reply is not answered blind.

## Telegram answers back

It used to be one-way, deliberately. That was the wrong call for the case that mattered: told at three in the morning that an account is waiting on a security check, on a machine at home, you could read it and do nothing.

Send `/help` for the list. It can tell you what is running, show you drafts that are waiting, approve or decline one, pause everything, and go quiet for a few hours.

The boundary moved rather than went away:

- **only the chat you paired.** Anything else is ignored without a reply.
- **a fixed list of commands.** Nothing reads a sentence, and nothing you send can reach an agent, a prompt, or a shell.
- **an approval from your phone passes the same checks** as one made in the app, because it is the same code.
- **nothing sensitive comes back.** No token, no keys, no cookies, no memory.
- **anything that changes something is recorded.** Who, what and when.

`/mute 4` silences the phone for a few hours and loses nothing, since everything is still raised and still in the app, and the settings screen says a mute is in force, so a bot that is connected and quiet is never a mystery.

## Smaller things

**A tab for each caller.** Three roles leasing at once is the ordinary startup, and the background-tab change in this release waited for "a page appeared" without checking which page: two callers could end up holding the same tab. Each tab now carries a one-off address and is claimed by it.

**A declined draft says who declined it.** Declining something held for review left no record at all, while approving one did. "Why was this never sent" is the question that row exists to answer.
