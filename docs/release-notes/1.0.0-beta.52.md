**Your agent now uses one careful budget for everything it asks X, backs off when X pushes back, and tells you which it is doing.** Watched accounts stay watched all the way through a decision, growth sessions really run, discovery prefers people with a real audience, and every shared owner surface says which agent it belongs to. Approvals no longer bounce back to review, and large queues can be decided in bounded batches.

## One budget for asking X anything

Every surface that reads X for an account used to keep its own schedule, and nothing added them up. Notifications, mention search, reply search, thread checks, watched accounts and watched keywords each looked reasonable on its own. Together, on one live account, they came to about six page loads a minute, and X started refusing searches while the rest carried on at full speed. That is also when the browser settled on a dark screen with the X logo.

Each account now has one budget for reading X, shared by the radar, the channel poller and the replies it resolves. It is spent in a fixed order:

1. People who wrote to your agent, and the reads a reply needs. These may use the whole budget.
2. Accounts you asked your agent to watch.
3. Everything your agent goes looking for on its own, which may use only the first part of the budget.

So when the account is busy, looking for strangers is what waits. A worker coming back after downtime no longer asks X for every source that fell due while it was off; broad discovery takes its share and waits for the rest.

The budget is part of the account's cadence, so it can be tuned per account. X publishes no number for how often a signed-in browser may load a page, so AI17Z does not claim one. The defaults are deliberately modest and leave headroom.

## When X pushes back

X saying "slow down", showing its own error page, or never finishing a page now counts as pressure on the account, not only on the one source that saw it.

- The first sign slows the account down. Broad discovery reads less, and your agent stops approaching new people. Replies continue.
- Pushback while already slowed, or a third "slow down" within the hour, starts a cooldown. Broad discovery and watched accounts pause, and nothing your agent would start on its own goes out until it ends. Replies to people who wrote in continue, at the account's usual spacing, and reading their thread never waits behind the monitors.
- A cooldown ends in a recovery period of the same length, not at full speed. Going looking for people waits until recovery is over.
- Each repeat doubles the next cooldown, to a ceiling of two hours by default. A clean recovery forgives one.

Nothing is retried in a loop. A held read waits for its moment, and a held action waits without using up an attempt. An action whose outcome was unclear is still checked on X before anything is tried again, exactly as before.

The Behaviour panel shows how many reads the account made in the last ten minutes and for whom, how often X pushed back in the last hour, and when a cooldown or recovery ends.

## A dark screen is not an empty feed

A page X never finished drawing, whether the logo on a dark screen or a spinner where the timeline should be, used to be recorded as a healthy poll that found nothing. It is now recorded as a failed read. The source's place in the feed does not move, so nothing it failed to read is skipped, and the stall counts as pressure on the account. A page where X says there is nothing to see is still an ordinary quiet answer.

## A watched account is not a stranger

Posts from an account you explicitly chose now carry their own `TARGET_ACCOUNT_ACTIVITY` meaning from the radar into the event, job, decision and audit trail. They no longer inherit the topic-match threshold or multi-day stranger cooldown meant for unsolicited outreach.

Every eligible post from a watched account is deliberately considered, and by default your agent replies. It still will not shadow somebody: once it has answered that account as many times in an hour as your agent answers any one person, further posts that hour are left alone with a sentence saying so, and the same goes for a fourth turn in one thread. Do-not-contact, permissions, account health, rate limits, duplicate prevention and exact-target checks all still apply.

Each target keeps the newest post it has seen and processed, and those only move forward, so a restart or an older post surfacing later does not lose its place. Every post has a durable disposition, and the Behaviour panel shows each target, its latest disposition and why.

## Your agent goes looking on its own

Until now, everything an agent replied to arrived because somebody mentioned it, replied to it, posted from an account you told it to watch, or matched a search you wrote. Its growth sessions opened, found nothing, and closed, so growth depended on being mentioned.

A new radar source, **Goes looking on its own**, lets your agent search X for itself once per growth session:

- **What it searches for.** The specific topics in its persona: names, tickers and products such as Robinhood Chain or $PONS, never vague words like "builders". You can pin exact searches instead.
- **Filtered by X itself.** Only English original posts that already have likes (`min_faves`) come back, and your agent keeps just the few from the biggest real audiences.
- **Personal subjects stay personal.** It never goes looking under faith, family or health, or any subject it may not raise unasked. It still talks about them when they come up.
- **Everything else still applies.** Quiet hours, the account's X budget and health, the outreach limits and the ordinary judgement of whether a post is worth answering.

## Choosing well, not choosing big

Ranked on audience alone, your agent's own searches put a 1.6-million-follower account flexing old calls and a 1.3-million one pitching a token at the top of the list, above a 55-thousand-follower account with a real thesis about its subject. Discovery now ranks on several things at once, and writes the reasons onto each post it keeps:

- **Whether the post names your agent's own topics**, which counts for most.
- **The author's audience and the post's traction**, log-scaled, so a million followers is not a thousand times a thousand.
- **Whether it is a token pitch.** Flexing past calls, multipliers, "sending", "bid zone", "don't miss this setup", giveaways and engagement farming mark a post down hard, and so do a contract address with little else said, a call teased through its contract ("I have the CA to the next..."), a price promised on a clock and a closing "want in?". A ticker on its own does not: most of what your agent should talk about has one.
- **Whom it has spoken to.** Somebody approached this week is marked down, somebody who approached once or twice and never answered is marked down further, and somebody who has written to your agent is marked up.

The same pitch and history checks apply to every post your agent comes across on its own, not only to its searches. None of it applies to somebody who writes to your agent.

## Rehearse a post the way your agent would really meet it

The Lab can now rehearse a real post as something your agent found on its own, or as a post from a watched account, as well as something somebody sent it. The three are held to different bars and written in different voices, so rehearsing one as another showed the wrong answer.

## Joining a conversation, not answering one

Every reply used to be written as an answer to somebody who had spoken to your agent. Under a post it found on its own, that produced explanations: the stranger's post summarised back to them, with its numbers repeated. Replies your agent makes unasked are now written as what they are: a reaction, one sharp question, a joke, or a point only somebody who knows the space would make. They are short, never speak for a project or team, and never promise anything on its behalf. Replies to people who wrote in are unchanged.

A watched account's one-word replies to other people ("yes", "+", "worst ever") are no longer treated as posts to join. They are recorded with a reason and left alone; the watched account's own posts and anything with a thought in it are still answered.

## Going looking, for people somebody reads

When X reports how many people follow the author of a post your agent came across, that now counts. An author with a real audience ranks higher, one almost nobody follows ranks lower, and a count X did not report counts for nothing either way. It is a ranking, not a cutoff. An owner who wants a floor can set the smallest audience worth approaching; it applies only to posts your agent found on its own and only when X reported the number.

Nothing about this ever applies to somebody who writes to your agent. A reply from an account with five followers is answered exactly as one from an account with half a million.

Every post your agent comes across on its own now has a record of whether it took a closer look or declined, and why. The Behaviour panel shows what it came across today, what it took, what it published, the typical audience of what it took, and the reason it declined most often.

## Growth sessions now exist in the real scheduler

The previous release added session and cooldown rules, but the scheduler only read those rules; it never opened the durable session row they depend on. Real installations had no growth-session history and could not truthfully enter cooldown.

Optional growth now opens a durable session before it thinks, records what the session used, closes on normal completion even when no candidate was found, and starts the configured cooldown. A worker restart with an abandoned open session is recovered from the ledger instead of leaving growth wedged. Sessions can now be paced by the hour, and the daily ceiling can be set to zero for no daily stop. The panel distinguishes a session that is open from an agent that is merely eligible and shows the next eligible time.

## Quiet hours are per agent

Quiet hours for going looking stay on by default, eleven at night to seven in the morning in the timezone you set. They can now be switched off for one agent, which changes nothing for any other agent and nothing else about pacing: cooldown, the hourly ceiling, the account's X budget and its health all still apply.

## Multi-agent Activity and Inbox

Activity and Inbox now scope by agent in the database before limits are applied. Choose All agents or one named agent in either screen; row counts and rows use the same scope, and the scope stays in the URL.

In All agents, every row names its agent. An event seen through a shared account can be represented once per relevant agent, including the case where one agent created a job and another did not.

Waiting for You also names the agent on every request. Its bounded attention window shares space across agents, so one noisy agent cannot consume the whole owner surface while direct, security and irreversible decisions keep their higher priority.

## Approval now advances the approved work

Approval used to requeue a job after validation, and the unchanged soft voice or quality verdict then sent the same draft straight back to review. Your approval now overrides that unchanged soft judgement, while hard policy, permissions, account state, rate limits and exact-target verification are still checked at execution. The action idempotency key still prevents a restart or a repeated approval from publishing twice.

## Select 10, 25, 50 or a bounded custom amount

Large review queues now support Select 10, Select 25, Select 50, a custom amount, Select all loaded, and Clear selection, following the rows already shown in the current scope and order.

One command may include up to 500 items. They are deduplicated and processed in chunks of 25, each through the ordinary ownership and policy checks, and the result reports decided, superseded, blocked and failed counts separately.

## Automated likes follow the growth policy

A growth policy that sets zero likes or reposts a day now switches them off, even where the capability is granted. The daily ceiling is the tighter of the policy and the built-in limit.

## Reading X when X has changed

- X now refuses its search endpoint to anything but its own app. Searches read the results X's own app fetched to draw the page, with no extra request and nothing imitated, so authors' audiences and posts' engagement come through again.
- X moved where it sends follower counts. Both the new and the old place are read, and a missing count stays missing.
- Finding X's operation ids no longer takes a neighbouring operation's id, and a refused read now says why in the log.
- A browser AI17Z has only just started gets one longer second attempt to attach on a busy machine, instead of failing the first time the handshake runs slow.
- Updating an installation to a new build of the same version now rebuilds its images, instead of running the new worker beside the old interface.

## Faster replies, with the same thinking

Measured over four days of real replies before this release, a reply took a median of 86 seconds from arriving to being ready, and 62 of those were spent reading the thread from X before any model ran. The model itself took under ten.

- **Reading a thread is one step, not a hundred.** The thread was read one article at a time, with several separate requests to the browser for each field of each article. It is now read in a single pass that returns exactly the same thing, and the old way remains as the fallback. The channel poller reads its feed the same way.
- **Pictures are described only for posts your agent is going to answer.** Over the same four days, 364 of 426 image descriptions were for posts your agent then declined. The decision whether to answer never looked at them, so the description now waits until that decision is made.
- **A plan that runs out of time is stopped, not left running.** When the model choosing what to look up missed its deadline, the reply stopped waiting but the request carried on and was paid for. It is now cancelled at the deadline. The same applies to your agent's own reflection.
- **A question about your agent is answered from your agent.** "What are you working on right now?" read as a question about current events and went to a web search and then to the research planner. Every question in that shape now skips both, because the answer lives in your agent's own memory and character. A question that names something checkable, such as "what is your take on the ETH price today?", is still looked up.
- **A slow thread read says where its time went.** Each reply's context step now records how long it waited for the browser tab, for X to draw the page, and for each part of the read, so a slow reply can be attributed rather than guessed at.
- **Thirty seconds that were a timeout, not X.** Those timings showed every thread read spending exactly thirty seconds looking for a link preview card, and sixty when the post above was read too. Most posts have no card, and the reader waited the full default timeout for one that was never coming. A page that has already drawn is now read as it is. Measured on a live installation, a thread read went from 32 to 62 seconds down to about 3. Web search results with no snippet had the same wait and the same fix.
- **Replies to people keep moving when the account is resting.** See above.

The Fast, Balanced and Thorough setting is unchanged in what it offers: Fast skips the voice rewrite and the research plan, Balanced is the default, Thorough waits longer for the plan. All three benefit from the changes above, because none of them were optional work.

## What two agents' own histories changed

Two agents have been running for weeks on a real installation, and their records were read before anything here changed. Every change below started with something measured in that history, and each has a test built on the shape of what happened, never the words anybody wrote.

- **Documents are retrieved when they are about the question.** An agent with documentation attached was pulling five or six passages into nearly every reply on words like "there", "only" and "one": "Goated times" brought back six release notes. Common words no longer count, a word most of the documentation shares no longer counts, and a passage now has to match a fair share of what the message is about, or one word rare enough to identify it. Replayed over the last few hundred replies, irrelevant passages fell by about two thirds, while passages from an agent's own subject documents still reach the posts about that subject.
- **An agent is told the phrases it keeps using.** One agent had used the same few three-word runs in five and six different replies, each too small an overlap with any single reply for the repetition check to notice. Phrases used in three or more recent replies are now named to the model before it writes, and a draft that reuses one is sent for a rewrite. Contractions and function words never count as a habit, and a signature phrase you set keeps its own rest period.
- **Relationships remember what you talked about.** The prompt could always say "You have discussed..." and nothing ever filled it in: eighty relationships, some forty exchanges deep, with no topics between them. Each published exchange now records up to three subjects both sides actually named. Never anything from somebody's timeline, never a handle or your agent's own name, and never a subject your agent would not raise unasked.
- **Asked about itself, an agent answers as itself.** An agent whose voice is modelled on a real founder was asked "what are you working on right now?" in a rehearsal and answered that it was heads down on the founder's product. Its persona forbade exactly that. A message asking the agent about itself now carries the rule with it: answer only from the agent's own state and what it has actually done on its account, never present the life or work of the person its voice is modelled on as its own, and say so plainly when nothing answers it.
- **Your agent is told how long its replies usually run.** At the Fast setting, which may not ask for a rewrite, drafts that ran well past an agent's usual length went to review. The model is now told the same length the draft will be judged against, before it writes.
- **Discovery spends its places on posts it can answer.** A search that kept its best three was losing two of them to the freshness window after the fact. Posts too old to answer are now set aside before ranking, and the radar says how many.
- **How your agent has been answering, on the Autonomy panel.** How many drafts it produced today, the typical and slowest time to a draft, model calls per answer, and any phrase it keeps reaching for.

Some of what the history showed was already fixed by the time it was read, and is listed so it is not fixed twice: the keyword flood that the owner rejected four hundred times stopped when discovery was triaged before any work was queued, and image descriptions for declined posts stopped when they were deferred.

## Upgrade notes

Two migrations. The first adds durable target state and per-post dispositions and preserves existing radar sources. The second adds the account's X capacity ledger, its breaker state, and the record of broad-discovery decisions. Existing watched accounts, agent state, browser profiles and signed-in sessions need no recreation. Existing accounts start healthy, with the default budget.
